On Computing for Insight: Notes from a Quantitative Education

"The purpose of computing is insight, not numbers." — Richard Hamming. A profile of the author, in his own words.

I am a Quantitative Finance graduate student at NYU Tandon with a background in Computational Finance and Risk Management (Data Science) from the University of Washington. My expertise lies in derivative pricing, stochastic modeling, and the application of machine learning within high-frequency financial contexts.

The work collected in this edition — from GARCH-simulated volatility surfaces to anomaly detection in millisecond order-book data — reflects a single preoccupation: building robust models that account for market ambiguity and tail-risk volatility.

§ I. Selected Research & Projects

FRE 6883 · C++

Earnings Announcements and the Anatomy of Abnormal Returns

A sector-neutral event study of quarterly earnings across the Russell 3000. Beat, Meet, and Miss cohorts are drawn from earnings surprise; AAR and CAAR paths are estimated with bootstrap sampling against the EOD Historical Data API.

PaperRepository
FRE 6083 · Stochastic Modeling

Two Views of Volatility: Black-Scholes Against GARCH(1,1)

Implied-volatility term structures for S&P 500 options, derived classically and by GARCH simulation. Three-dimensional surfaces reveal Black-Scholes overpricing deep out-of-the-money calls by as much as 8.6 percent.

Report
FRE 6123 · Risk

Measures of Ruin: VaR, CVaR, and the Discipline of Stress

A comprehensive treatment of Value-at-Risk estimation, conditional VaR, stress testing, and regulatory capital frameworks spanning market, credit, and operational exposures.

PaperReport
FRE 7773 · Machine Learning

Spoofing, Layering, and the Forest That Finds Them

Seventy-six session-aware features over millisecond limit-order-book data feed an Isolation Forest trained on normal sessions, flagging manipulative patterns from spoofing to quote stuffing.

Report
Fixed Income · AI Review

Correcting the Machines: Treasury Analytics Under Review

Large-language-model drafts are audited against fixed-income pricing requirements; the corrected implementation covers 32nds quote parsing, actual/actual accrual, Newton-solved yield, PV01, duration, and convexity.

ReportCode
Research · FX

Ambiguity Abroad: Do Central Banks Seek Safe Havens?

A quarterly FX ambiguity index built from nine major currency pairs, merged with IMF COFER reserve data. Regression over 37 quarters finds a weak negative relation (r = −0.36): reserve managers, unlike private carry traders, do not flee to USD and CHF when ambiguity rises.

Manuscript in preparation
